Privacy policy

Mizar is built so that the project never receives your data. This policy says exactly what the software does, in plain words first and precisely second.

Version
1.0
Effective
24 November 2026

This page describes how the Mizar software and this website behave. It is not legal advice.

In short

  • We collect nothing. There is no Mizar server, no account and no analytics.
  • Your screen, input, clipboard and files go directly between your own devices, encrypted.
  • Keys, pairings, settings and logs stay on your devices.
  • Remote access is off until you turn it on. Then public services see an internet address, never what you do.
  • Google sign-in is optional and not released yet. It keeps one encrypted list in a private folder of your own Google Drive, and cannot see your other files.
  • This website sets no cookies and loads nothing from anywhere else.

Who this policy is from, and what it covers

"Mizar" is remote control software for Windows and Android, developed by the Mizar project ("the project", "we"). This policy covers the Mizar apps and this website.

Mizar is in private development. Some features described here, Google sign-in in particular, are designed but not yet released. They are described as designed, so that this policy is accurate on the day they ship. If the design changes, this policy changes first.

You can reach the project through the project's issue page.

What the project collects

Nothing. The project operates no server that the apps talk to. We have no user accounts, no database of users or devices, no analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting and no advertising. We could not look up your devices or your sessions if we were asked to, because that information never reaches us.

What stays on your devices

Mizar keeps the following on the devices where you install it, and does not upload it:

DataPurposeWhere it is kept
The device's own cryptographic keysTo prove the device's identity to your other devices and to encrypt sessionsWindows: %ProgramData%\Mizar (a PC that can be controlled) and %LOCALAPPDATA%\Mizar (per Windows account). Android: the app's private storage
The list of paired devices (name, kind, public key)To know which devices may connectThe same places
Settings, key-mapping profiles, last known addresses of your paired devicesTo work the way you set it up and to reconnect quicklyThe same places
LogsTo diagnose problems on that deviceThe same places. Logs hold events, counts, sizes and error reasons. They do not hold screen content, typed input or clipboard content, and file names appear only if you turn on detailed logging
Files you receiveBecause you transferred themYour Downloads folder, in a subfolder named Mizar

You can delete all of it. The Windows uninstaller asks whether to keep or remove this data; on Android, uninstalling the app removes the app's private storage.

Sessions between your devices

When one of your devices controls another, they exchange the screen picture, sound, keyboard, mouse and touch input, and control messages. This travels directly between the two devices and is encrypted end to end with keys created at pairing, which never leave the devices. There is no relay: no third party carries your session, in encrypted form or otherwise.

A device can connect only after it has been paired, and pairing needs a person at the controlled device to allow it. A paired device can see the screen, send input, sync the clipboard and transfer files as described below. It cannot run arbitrary commands.

Clipboard and file transfer

Clipboard sync and file transfer happen only inside a session, only between the two paired devices, and inside the same encrypted connection. Nothing is uploaded to any service.

Clipboard content that its source marks as sensitive (as password managers do) is not synced and not read, unless you enable that yourself. Received files are saved to your Downloads folder and are never opened automatically. The controlled PC shows the person using it that a remote device listed, fetched or sent files, with counts and sizes.

Remote access through public infrastructure

Remote access (reaching a PC from another network) is off by default. While it is off, a Mizar PC contacts no public service.

When you turn it on, Mizar uses public services that are not operated by the project to let your paired devices find each other. They are used only for the introduction, never for the session.

ServiceWhat it is used forWhat it can seeWhat it cannot see
Public STUN serversTo tell a device its own public addressThe device's IP address and port when it asksAnything else. The request carries only a random number
Public Nostr relaysTo pass small sealed notes between your two devices ("here is where I am right now")The device's IP address; throwaway keys that are derived per relay and change every day; the time and the (fixed) size of the notesThe content of the notes; your name, account, device names or device keys; the same pair of devices on a different relay
The BitTorrent Mainline DHTTo store a small sealed presence record, and as a slow fallbackThe IP address of whoever stores or reads the record; a record address that changes every day; ciphertextThe content; which devices are behind the record
Your own routerTo open one inbound port, named "Mizar", if you allow automatic port forwardingThat the PC asked for the portThe session content

Things worth knowing:

  • These services receive your device's IP address because that is how the internet works. An IP address can indicate your approximate location and your internet provider.
  • A relay that your devices use on consecutive days can tell that "the same anonymous pair as yesterday" is back. It cannot tell who the pair is.
  • The services are run by third parties under their own terms. Mizar ships a list of them, uses several from different operators at once, and lets you turn the public services off.
  • Pairing with a one-time code uses the same Nostr relays to carry a sealed exchange. The code itself is never sent.
  • Your internet provider and networks along the path can see that your devices exchange encrypted traffic, as with any encrypted connection.

Turning remote access off stops all contact with these services at once.

Signing in with Google

Status: designed, not yet released. Signing in is optional. Mizar works without it, with devices paired by hand.

Why Mizar asks

So that you can sign in on a new device and see the devices you already own, without pairing each one by hand. Mizar has no server on which to keep that list, so it is kept in your own Google account, in a place only Mizar can use.

What Mizar accesses

Permission (scope)Google user data accessedHow Mizar uses it
openidA sign-in token containing a stable identifier of your Google accountTo recognise that the same Google account signed in on each of your devices
emailYour Google account's e-mail addressTo show you, in the app, which account you are signed in with. Mizar does not send e-mail
https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.appdataOnly the files that Mizar itself created in its hidden application data folder in your Google DriveTo store and read the list of your devices

The Drive permission is limited to Mizar's own application data folder. Mizar cannot see, read, change or delete any other file in your Google Drive, and does not ask for any other Google data: no contacts, mail, calendar, photos or profile beyond the above.

What is stored in the folder

The list of your devices: for each device a name, its kind, its public keys, the record of which of your devices approved it, and which public meeting points it uses. The list is encrypted with a key that only your approved devices hold, so Google stores it but cannot read it. The device list never contains screen content, files, passwords or session history.

Signing in is two short steps: Google's own consent screen, then one confirmation in Mizar before a new device is trusted. Your Google account alone cannot add a device.

Who receives Google user data

  • The Mizar project: nothing. There is no Mizar server. Your Google identifier, e-mail address, tokens and files never reach the project.
  • Third parties: nothing. Mizar does not sell, rent or transfer Google user data to anyone, and does not use it for advertising, for profiling, for credit decisions, or to develop, improve or train AI or machine-learning models.
  • Your own devices: when you add a device, its sign-in token is shown to your other Mizar devices so that they can verify, using Google's public signing keys, that the new device belongs to the same Google account. This happens between your own devices only.
  • People: no person at the project can read your Google user data, because the project never has it.

How it is protected

  • On Windows, the token that keeps you signed in is stored in your Windows user profile, encrypted with Windows data protection for your account. On Android, sign-in is handled by Google's own sign-in service on the phone and Mizar does not store a long-lived token.
  • A PC that is controlled unattended holds no Google token at all.
  • All communication with Google uses HTTPS. The device list is encrypted before it is uploaded, as described above.

How long it is kept, and how to delete it

Mizar keeps this data only while you use the feature, and you can remove it at any time:

  • Sign out in Mizar (Settings, on each device): removes the sign-in from that device.
  • Remove Mizar's access in your Google Account, under Security > Your connections to third-party apps and services > Mizar > Delete all connections: all your devices are signed out and Mizar can no longer reach your account.
  • Delete the stored list in Google Drive on the web, under Settings > Manage apps > Mizar > Options > Delete hidden app data.
  • Uninstall Mizar and choose "remove everything": deletes the local copies.

Because the project holds none of this data, there is nothing for us to delete on our side. If you need help with any of these steps, ask on the project's issue page.

What Google can see

Google, as the provider of your account, sees each sign-in and token refresh (with IP address and time), and the files in the folder (as ciphertext: their sizes, number and timing). A controlled PC may fetch Google's public signing keys, which shows Google an IP address, as with any app that verifies Google sign-ins. Google does not see your sessions, which devices connect to which, or their addresses. Google's handling of your account is covered by Google's own privacy policy.

Limited Use

Mizar's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

If Mizar's use of Google user data ever changes, this policy will be updated first, and the app will ask for your consent before using the data in a new way.

This website

This website is a set of static pages. It sets no cookies, contains no analytics, no advertising and no embedded third-party content, and makes no requests to other hosts. If you use the light/dark switch, or dismiss the note that offers another language, that choice is kept in your browser's local storage on your device and is never transmitted. The note appears when your browser's language setting differs from the page; the site never redirects you.

The pages are hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub processes the IP address of each request in order to deliver the page, and may log it under GitHub's privacy statement. The project does not receive these logs.

Children

Mizar is a general-purpose tool and is not directed at children. The project collects no personal data from anyone, including children.

Security

All connections between Mizar devices are encrypted and authenticated with keys that stay on the devices. A controlled device ignores devices it has not been paired with. No software is free of defects; if you find a security problem, please report it through the project's issue page without publishing details that would put others at risk.

Your choices

  • Use Mizar on your local network only: leave remote access off.
  • Use remote access without public services: turn the public services off in the remote access settings.
  • Do not sign in with Google: pair devices by hand or with a one-time code instead.
  • Remove a device: delete it from "Paired devices" on the device it was paired with.
  • Remove everything: uninstall and choose "remove everything".

Depending on where you live, you may have legal rights to access, correct or delete personal data that an organisation holds about you. The project holds none; the data described here is on your own devices and in your own Google account, where you control it directly.

Changes to this policy

When the software's behaviour changes in a way that affects this policy, the policy is updated before the change is released, the version and date at the top change, and the change is listed below. Changes that affect how Google user data is used are also announced in the app, and require your consent where they extend that use.
